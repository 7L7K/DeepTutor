# Reading excerpt — Why cells use more than one energy strategy

Cellular respiration is a linked set of reactions rather than one reaction at
one location. Glycolysis begins in the cytosol and divides one glucose into
two pyruvate molecules. Pyruvate oxidation and the citric acid cycle prepare
high-energy electrons for the membrane-bound electron transport chain.

NADH and FADH2 carry those electrons to the chain. As electrons move through
the chain, proton pumping creates a gradient across the inner mitochondrial
membrane. ATP synthase uses the gradient to make ATP. In aerobic respiration,
oxygen receives the electrons at the end of the chain and combines with
protons to form water.

Fermentation is a different way to preserve glycolysis when the aerobic chain
cannot accept electrons. It regenerates NAD+ from NADH. Because fermentation
does not use the electron transport chain for oxidative phosphorylation, its
ATP yield per glucose is much smaller. The shared beginning is important:
fermentation keeps glycolysis operating, but it does not complete the aerobic
pathway.
