# Lecture 6 transcript — Cellular respiration

**[00:00:00] Instructor:** Today we will follow one glucose molecule through
cellular respiration. The pathway has four teaching stages: glycolysis,
pyruvate oxidation, the citric acid cycle, and the electron transport chain
with oxidative phosphorylation.

**[00:03:12] Instructor:** Glycolysis occurs in the cytosol and splits glucose
into two pyruvate molecules. It also produces a small amount of ATP and
reduces NAD+ to NADH.

**[00:06:40] Instructor:** Pyruvate oxidation converts pyruvate to acetyl-CoA.
The citric acid cycle then oxidizes the acetyl group and captures energy in
NADH and FADH2 while releasing carbon dioxide.

**[00:10:18] Instructor:** The electron transport chain is embedded in the
inner mitochondrial membrane. Electrons from NADH and FADH2 drive proton
pumping. The resulting proton gradient powers ATP synthase.

**[00:13:05] Instructor:** Oxygen is the terminal electron acceptor at the end
of the aerobic electron transport chain. Oxygen accepts electrons and protons
to form water. Without that final acceptor, electron flow through the chain
cannot continue normally.

**[00:16:22] Instructor:** Fermentation does not replace glycolysis. It lets a
cell keep glycolysis running by regenerating NAD+. Fermentation therefore uses
no oxygen as a terminal electron acceptor and produces much less ATP per
glucose than aerobic respiration.
